# Nullify privacy policy

_Last updated: 13 October 2026. Applies to the Nullify browser extension, version 0.1.0 and later._

**Nullify does not collect, sell, share or send any of your data.** It has no account, no analytics, no ads, and no servers.

## What Nullify does in your browser
To protect your clicks, Nullify's script runs on the pages you visit and watches what a click tries to do: open a pop-up, send the tab to another site, or land on something invisible. All of this happens inside your browser. Nullify doesn't send page contents, addresses or anything else off your device, and it doesn't load any code from the internet.

## What Nullify keeps, and where
| What | Where | How long |
|---|---|---|
| The sites where you switched Nullify off | Your browser's extension storage (`storage.local`) | Until you switch the site back on or remove Nullify |
| The list of what was blocked in each tab: the page, what tried to open, which script did it, and when | Your browser's memory (`storage.session`) | Cleared when you close the tab or the browser. Capped at the last 5 pages per tab |

Nothing in either list leaves your browser. Removing the extension deletes both.

## Permissions
- **Access to the sites you visit:** hijacking traps can be on any site, so the protection has to run on every page.
- **Scripting:** to start the protection on each page before the page's own scripts run.
- **Storage:** for the two lists above.
- **Web navigation:** to start a fresh list when a tab loads a new page, and to close a tab that opens to a site Nullify just blocked in that tab.
